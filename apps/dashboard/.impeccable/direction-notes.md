# Visual direction study

Mode: Operate. The user delegates all interview and design answers. Keep the installed skill for this task; do not update tooling mid-redesign.

Mechanism: a product-scoped typed event store turns anonymous website/tool activity into trends, aggregates, and inspectable events.
Scene: a developer switches between their editor and analytics during the day, then checks a release from a phone; Auto theme follows their environment.
Cultural home: tools for making, measuring, and debugging, with clear instruments rather than sales dashboards.
Ruts excluded: blue SaaS card grid; its predictable opposite, black neon terminal chrome.

Seven candidate systems, ordered by resonance:
1. Scientific plotting workbench — precise axes, ink text, aubergine selection, flat measurement rows; lends authority to numeric evidence without corporate styling.
2. Transit operations board — amber controls, graphite text, segmented status lanes; makes product/date scope and current state immediate.
3. Desktop publishing tool palette — white and raspberry, compact contextual toolbars, generous live canvas; emphasizes constructing queries and inspecting output.
4. Swiss public wayfinding — signal red, white, charcoal, strong naming and compact aligned signs; prioritizes orientation across three destinations.
5. Botanical field station labels — cool stone, dark pine, muted ochre, clean specimen rows; expresses deliberate observation and privacy without faux paper texture.
6. Japanese measurement-instrument manuals — white, graphite, persimmon, crisp tabular measurements, robust control groupings; makes every datum readable at a glance.
7. Audio mixing console — graphite neutrals, muted magenta selection, stacked horizontal channels; supports live state and dense event inspection without glowing decoration.

The candidates span scientific graphics, civic information systems, publishing interfaces, physical records, and studio equipment. No literal props, ornamental textures, or invented analytics features.

## Resolution

Seed: 1c47a4fc, assigned candidate 5. Build the field-station label system, with a compact query rail above a shared result field. User delegated the final choice.

Challengers evaluated on audience identification and product clarity:
- Developer console: high identification / high clarity, but preserves too much of the rejected app-kit world; field station gives the same precision with a distinct daytime identity.
- Botanical folio: medium identification / low clarity; the illustrated lifecycle staging invents relationships Piko does not model.
- Type specimen: medium identification / low clarity; gigantic glyph manipulation displaces analytics rather than explaining it.
  The map, mixtape, and mascot catalog remain outside the hand: their distinctive controls require actions or authored product content absent from this tool.

Staging: registered shared measurements, from the folio's common-scale principle, with a plot and categorical breakdown on one field; individual events follow directly below. The finite tile editor, floor directory, and annotation light-table imply editing or spatial navigation that Piko does not provide.

Color strategy: restrained. Stone neutrals and pine for action/data; a pale citron navigation region anchors the workspace. In daylit development work the light theme stays crisp; Auto and dark respect evening use without adding glow or neon.
